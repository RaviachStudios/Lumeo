The read

It's a real complaint, not a scam or a bot false-positive. Hasbro genuinely owns SIMON —Milton Bradley, filed 1980, still live). The complainant is outside counsel with amatching law-firm domain. Google isn't asserting the violation itself; it's forwarding a third-party notice and putting the burden on you.

Two things are being complained about, not one:
1. The word "Simon" in your app title.
2. The four-colour quadrant disc — that's a trade-dress claim on the classic look, which is why they linked the Amazon product page.

Fixing only #1 leaves #2 standing. Both need to move.

The clock: 7 days from the email, then "we may need to restrict the availability of this app" — that's a takedown, and repeated policy strikes escalate toward account-level action. What date did this land?

The realistic path

Contacting the complainant is what Google suggests first, but Hasbro's counsel is not going to license SIMON to an indie memory game. Don't burn days waiting on that. Rebrand. The underlying game mechanic — repeat a growing sequence of lights and tones — is not protected by trademark; only the name and the look are. You can keep the game.

What has to change: app title, icon, feature graphic, screenshots, store description, anppearance. Developer name is fine — "Raviach Studios" isn't at issue.

One thing to decide early: com.raviachstudios.simon cannot be changed on an existing listing. Changing it means publishing a brand-new app and losing every install, review and ranking signal. Google's own remediation list doesn't mention the package ID, and it isn't visible in the store UI, so keeping it is the normal outcome — but it does appear in the URL. Worth a line in your reply to Google asking them to confirm the ID can stay.

Your skin system helps here. You already ship non-classic wheels — volcano, wild west, and the rest. The exposed surface is essentially the default/classic skin and the store art built around it. Retiring or restyling that one default is a much smaller job than it sounds.
